# Cost model: every released FOIA record, stored and searchable

Draft, 2026-11-17. Generated by `python scripts/cost_model.py`. Every input is an editable
assumption in that script, with its rationale. The scope is third-party releases only:
first-party records (A-files, medical, personnel) are never public. Phase 2/3, where the system
files requests for every requestable record, is out of scope.

What each row means:
* **historic**: everything released from FY1998 to FY2025. It assumes all of it can be obtained,
  which is not realistic in year one. Getting the records, through follow-up requests and agency
  processing, is the real bottleneck, not money.
* **future**: new third-party releases each year.
* **storage**: originals on Backblaze B2 at $6.95/TB-month, plus a second copy in S3 Glacier
  Deep Archive at $0.99/TB-month.
* **OCR**: cloud OCR at $1.50 per 1,000 pages (Google Document AI, Textract or Azure Read),
  or self-hosted Tesseract at about $0.05 per 1,000 pages.
* **search server**: a dedicated NVMe machine that serves the full-text index.
* **year-1 totals**: a deliberate upper bound. They charge full-backfill storage for all 12 months.

## What the numbers say

1. **Storage is not the expensive part.** Even the mid case, 48 TB of originals, costs about
   $380/month with a deep-archive second copy. Today's archive is a few GB and grows about
   1.35 GB/week (the Nov 2026 probe), so it costs well under $1/month once billing is on.
   The current B2 failures look like the free 10 GB cap, not a lack of money. Adding a payment
   method and raising the B2 cap fixes them; check with `main.py b2-diagnose --write-probe`.
2. **OCR is the largest one-time cost, and the method matters 30×.** Mid case: about $500k
   with cloud OCR versus about $17k self-hosted. Self-hosted Tesseract is already in the
   pipeline. Cloud OCR is worth it only for pages Tesseract handles badly.
3. **Searchability means hosting a full-text index.** The mid case needs about 3.6 TB of
   index, which outgrows free Render and SQLite on one disk. Plan on $60–400/month for a
   dedicated search server.
4. **Biggest uncertainty: pages per release.** No government-wide figure exists. The new FOIA
   log pipeline records `pages_released` per request; run
   `python scripts/cost_model.py --db <catalog.db>` to replace the guess with real data
   once 100 or more logged releases have page counts.
